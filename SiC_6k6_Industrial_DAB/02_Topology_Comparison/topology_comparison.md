Observations: at d = 1 (800/400 V) the SPS current is trapezoidal, reactive power is minimal and ZVS holds down to light load. At the corners (d = 0.71 and 1.38) SPS loses ZVS on one bridge below roughly half load and the rms current rises by ~50 %; this is the known SPS weakness that DPS/TPS modulation (03_Mathematical_Models, 11_Control_Algorithms) mitigates. The phase shift stays below 60 deg at every corner, so full power is reachable everywhere with control margin.

## 4. CLLLC resonant alternative

| required gain | f/f_r (FHA) | achievable |
|---|---|---|
| 0.71 | 2.00 | no (max gain limited) |
| 1.00 | 1.00 | yes |
| 1.38 | 0.48 | yes |

With Ln = 5 the FHA gain cannot reach 1.38 at acceptable Q, and reaching 0.71 requires operation far above resonance (loss of ZVS on the LV bridge, large circulating current, poor regulation). A CLLLC is excellent for a fixed ratio (e.g. 800/400 V battery-less DC transformer) but not for a 0.71-1.38 ratio window with bidirectional regulation.

## 6. Decision

**Selected: single-phase dual active bridge (DAB)** with SiC full bridges on both ports, n = 2, external series inductor plus transformer leakage, SPS modulation as the control baseline and DPS/TPS as the efficiency-extension modes. The PSFB is retained as the comparative baseline (`Legacy_PSFB_Baseline/`). The two-phase interleaved DAB is the fallback if the single-phase device/thermal budget (13_Thermal_Analysis) does not close; it is not needed at 6.6 kW with 32 mOhm / 16 mOhm 1200 V devices (see 04_Design_Calculations).
